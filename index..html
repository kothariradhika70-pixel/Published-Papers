Fear of Missing out (FOMO): Who is more likely to experience it?

Well, we all have experienced FOMO once. But the experience of FOMO differs from person to person. Nowadays young adults have increased access to social media where they can get a glimpse into the lives of their peers. With such a lifestyle also come the negative emotions associated with missing out on events and comparing oneself to others. This study explored the relationship between FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) and variables like Tendency to Maximize, Tendency to Regret and Social Comparison.

Individual Differences in Compliance to COVID-19 protocols: Importance of a plurality of messages in communication

Despite the prevention guidelines in place, the compliance rates among individuals differ. In order to improve voluntary compliance with the COVID-19 prevention guidelines, it is important to examine the personality attributes owing to the individual differences in compliance rates. The study explored the relationship between Compliance with COVID-19 prevention guidelines and Risk Propensity, Locus of Control, and Intolerance of Uncertainty respectively. The findings suggest the importance of a plurality of messages in communication by the experts and officials.

Impact of Sova Health Precision Nutrition Program on Metabolic Parameters: A retrospective study

The Sova Health Precision Nutrition Program is a fully digital remote outpatient care program aimed at improving treatment outcomes for chronic metabolic conditions (CMC). The program is based on the realization that CMCs stem from years of multiple small nutritional and lifestyle choices on a daily basis. Sova Health's proprietary algorithm identifies the deleterious everyday habits unique to each patient and suggests high-impact interventions. A compliance of 90% and beyond was considered good adherence to the program. The program combines behavioral nudges and scientific interventions to build healthy lifestyle habits associated with significant results in metabolic parameters.

The Socio-ecological Psychology of Financial Debts: The Role of Residential Mobility

Ever wondered how your neighborhood might influence your spending habits? Our study dives into the fascinating link between where you live and what's in your wallet. We explored the complex relationship between household debts, residential mobility, and cultural differences, revealing key insights for policymakers, financial institutions, and individuals. We discovered that American households generally have higher debt rates than Japanese ones. Across 76 countries, we found that household debts were higher in places where people move more frequently. This pattern held true in the U.S. too, with residents of more mobile cities carrying more credit card debt than those in stable cities. Our findings were consistent across 366 districts in India. We also found that Americans are more willing to borrow than Japanese, partly due to their history of moving frequently. Lastly, we discovered that people who move often feel less indebtedness or gratitude, influencing their credit card debts and borrowing behaviors.
